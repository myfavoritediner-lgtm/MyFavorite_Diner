# Admin Guide

**For the people who run My Favorite Diner.**

This guide explains how to manage the website yourself — bookings, the menu,
prices, photos, reviews, opening hours and promotional emails. You do not need
to know anything about code, and you cannot break the website by using this
panel.

Take your time. Nothing here happens instantly except sending a promotion,
and that asks you to confirm first.

## Signing in

1. Go to your website address followed by **`/admin`**.
   For example: `https://yourdomain.com/admin`
2. Enter the **email address** and **password** for your admin account.
3. Press **Sign in**.

That is the only way in. There is no public "register" or "sign up" — accounts
are created for you, which is deliberate.

**Bookmark the `/admin` page.** It is not linked from the website anywhere, on
purpose, so visitors never stumble into it.

> **Keep your password private.** Anyone who has it can change prices, read
> every booking and email your whole mailing list. If several people need
> access, give each of them their own account rather than sharing one —
> see [Adding or removing staff](#adding-or-removing-staff).

**Signing out:** use the sign-out button in the navigation. On a phone, open
the menu first.

## The Home screen

This is what you land on after signing in. Reading down the page:

- **The numbers at the top** — today's bookings, what needs a reply, and how
  the week is going.
- **A red alert bar**, but only when something is genuinely wrong (for
  example, email has stopped working). No bar means nothing needs you.
- **Daily limit** — how many tables you accept per day. See
  [Bookings](#bookings).
- **A seven-day chart** of bookings.
- **System monitor** — a health check of the website. Green is fine. Details
  stay hidden behind *Show details* so the page is not noisy.
- **What's been happening** — a plain-English list of everything recent: new
  bookings, cancellations, menu edits, people joining the mailing list.

The navigation across the top (or in the dropdown on a phone) takes you to
everything else: **Bookings, Menu, Gallery, Reviews, Subscribers, Promotions,
Settings**.

## Bookings

**Bookings** shows every table request. Use the filters at the top:

| Filter | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Today** | Tables booked for today |
| **Upcoming** | Everything still to come |
| **Needs a reply** | New requests nobody has dealt with yet — start here |
| **Past** | History |

There is a search box for finding someone by name or phone number.

### Dealing with a booking

Each booking moves through three stages:

1. **New** — the guest has asked for a table. Nobody has replied yet.
2. **Confirmed** — you are holding the table for them.
   **Marking a booking Confirmed automatically emails the guest**, so you do
   not need to phone or message them separately.
3. **Done** — they came in, or the date has passed.

You can also **cancel** a booking. The guest is told.

### The daily limit

**Home → Daily limit** sets how many tables you accept per day. The default is
**5**. Once a date reaches that number, the website stops offering it and
guests are asked to pick another day.

Cancelled bookings do not count, so cancelling frees the slot straight away.

### Guests cancelling themselves

Every confirmation email contains a cancel link. If a guest uses it, the table
is freed automatically and you are told — by LINE and by email. You do not
have to do anything.

## The menu

**Menu** is where prices, dishes, descriptions and photos live. Everything you
change here appears on the website immediately.

The page shows **one section at a time** — Burgers, Sandwiches, Hot Dogs and
so on — using the same tabs as the website. The search box above them looks
through every dish at once, by name or description.

### Changing a price or description

1. Open the section the dish is in.
2. Press the dish. A panel slides over the page.
3. Change the price, name, description or badge.
4. Press **Save**.

### Adding a dish

1. Open the right section.
2. Press **Add dish**.
3. Fill in at least a **name** and a **price**. Everything else is optional.
4. Press **Save**.

### Hiding a dish temporarily

Press the **eye** icon on the row. The dish disappears from the website but
keeps all its details, so you can bring it back with one press when it is
available again. This is what to use when something is off for a few days —
it is much better than deleting it.

### Reordering

Use the **up and down arrows**. The order you see is the order guests see.

### Sections

You can add a section, rename it, move it, hide it, or give it the small print
that appears under the heading — for example *"All burgers served with
shoestring fries"*.

> **Deleting a section deletes every dish inside it.** The button tells you
> how many before you agree. If you only want it off the website for a while,
> hide it instead.

## Photos and images

Photos are used in three places: **dishes** (Menu), the **photo grid**
(Gallery), and **promotion posters** (Promotions).

### Uploading a dish photo

1. **Menu** → open the dish.
2. Press **Upload** and choose the file from your computer or phone.
3. It appears in the preview straight away. Press **Save**.

You have three options for any dish photo:

- **Upload your own** — the best choice. The file is stored with the website
  and stays forever.
- **Pick one already included** — the site ships with around 110 dish
  photographs from the printed menu.
- **Paste a link** — works, but see the warning below.

### The photo grid

**Gallery** manages the photos on the homepage. You can add photos, remove
them, and change how large each tile is.

### A few practical tips

- **Landscape photos work best.** Very tall photos get cropped.
- **Photograph food in daylight** where you can — near a window beats the
  ceiling lights.
- **Very large files are slow for guests on phones.** If your camera produces
  an enormous file, any free "resize image" tool is worth a minute.

> **Avoid pasting Facebook photo links.** They stop working after about a
> week — Facebook expires them — and the photo will quietly vanish from your
> website. **Upload the file instead** and it stays for good. If a photo you
> added earlier has disappeared, this is almost always why: find it in Menu or
> Gallery and upload the file.

## Reviews

Guests can leave a review on your homepage. **Nothing they write appears on
the website until you approve it.**

When a review arrives you get a card in your LINE group, and a yellow banner
appears on Home telling you how many are waiting.

1. Go to **Reviews**.
2. Read the review.
3. Press **Approve** to put it on the homepage, **Hide** to keep it out of
   sight, or **Delete** to remove it entirely.

You can also **type in reviews yourself** — copied from Google, Facebook or
TripAdvisor. Those go live immediately, because you added them deliberately.

You can edit, reorder or remove any review later.

## Opening hours and contact details

**Settings** holds the details that appear all over the website:

- Phone number
- Email address
- Address and Google Maps link
- Opening hours
- **Days you are closed**

**The closed days matter most.** Any day you tick here is struck through on
the booking calendar and cannot be chosen, and the website says so in plain
words. You must leave at least one day open.

Changing your phone number or address here also updates what Google shows for
your restaurant, automatically.

## The mailing list

**Subscribers** is everyone who has signed up for news from the diner.

- People join themselves through the website, and get a welcome email.
- You can **add someone by hand** — for a guest who asks in person.
- You can **unsubscribe** someone.
- You can **copy all the addresses** if you need them elsewhere.

> Only add people who actually asked to hear from you. Emailing people who did
> not ask is the fastest way to have your emails treated as spam — which then
> affects your booking confirmations too.

## Sending a promotion

Promotions are **poster-first**: you design the artwork however you like
(Canva, a phone app, a designer) and the website sends it as the email.

1. **Promotions → New promotion.**
2. **Upload the poster.**
3. Write the **subject line** — this is what people see in their inbox, so
   make it count. The optional preview text appears just underneath it.
4. Optionally add a headline and a few words below the poster. Leave them
   empty to send the poster on its own.
5. **Send a test to yourself first.** Always do this. Open it on your phone
   and check it looks right.
6. When you are happy, **send to all subscribers**.

Everyone receives their own unsubscribe link, which is required by law and by
Gmail.

**It is safe to press send twice.** If your connection drops or you press the
button again, nobody gets the email twice — the website keeps track of who has
already received it.

Once a promotion has been sent it is locked and cannot be edited. Create a new
one instead.

> **A note on limits.** The email plan included allows around 3,000 emails a
> month and 100 a day. If your list grows beyond that, the plan needs
> upgrading before you can mail everyone at once.

## Changing your password

Your password lives with **Supabase**, the service that stores the website's
data. The admin panel has no "forgot password" link, so this is the way:

1. Sign in at [supabase.com](https://supabase.com) with the account that owns
   the project.
2. Open the project, then **Authentication** in the left sidebar.
3. Choose **Users**.
4. Find the email address, open the row's menu (**…**), and choose the option
   to reset or update the password.
5. Use the new password next time you sign in at `/admin`.

**If you are locked out** and cannot get into Supabase either, contact whoever
set the website up — the account recovery goes through Supabase's own email,
not through your website.

> **Good habits:** use a long password you do not use anywhere else, and never
> send it by LINE or email. If a staff member leaves, remove their account
> rather than changing one shared password.

## Adding or removing staff

Each person should have their own login. This is a two-step job and needs the
Supabase dashboard:

1. In Supabase → **Authentication → Users → Add user**, enter their email and
   a password, and tick *Auto Confirm User*.
2. Then add them to the staff list so the panel will let them in. In Supabase
   → **SQL Editor**, run this, replacing the two values:

   ```sql
   insert into public.staff (user_id, email) values ('their-user-id', 'their-email');
   ```

   Their user ID is shown on the Users page.

**To remove someone,** delete their row from that staff list — their access
stops immediately. Deleting their Supabase user account as well is tidier.

If someone signs in and sees **"Not your panel"**, it means step 2 has not
been done for them.

> Anyone on the staff list can change prices, read every booking and email
> your whole mailing list. Keep the list short, and take people off it when
> they leave.

## If something looks wrong

**Start at Home.** If there is no red bar at the top, the website believes it
is healthy.

For more detail, open **Settings → System status**. It checks the database,
email, booking alerts and everything else, and explains any problem in plain
English.

Common things and what they mean:

| What you see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| *No emails are arriving* | The email service needs attention — check System status |
| *Emails only reach my own address* | The sending domain has not been verified yet |
| A photo has vanished from the site | It was added as a Facebook link, which expired. Upload the file instead |
| **"Not your panel"** after signing in | That account is not on the staff list yet |
| A promotion says *already being sent* | A previous send was interrupted. It sorts itself out within the hour |

If none of that helps, contact whoever maintains the website and tell them
what you saw on the **System status** page — that is the page that tells them
the most.
